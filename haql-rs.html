What it is

A black-oil simulator small enough to audit.

Three-phase black oil (oil, water, gas), finite-volume TPFA on a Cartesian grid, fully implicit time stepping, Newton with a coloured Jacobian verified against dense finite differences. SPE1's own PVTO, PVDG, PVTW, SWOF and SGOF tables, rock compressibility, gravity, and Peaceman wells with rate control that switches to bottom-hole-pressure control when the limit binds. No framework, no licence server, no GPU — NumPy and SciPy.

Goes in

A simple Cartesian Eclipse deck — DIMENS, DX/DY/DZ, PERMX/Y/Z, PORO, TOPS — plus the PVT and saturation tables. FIELD units throughout. Corner-point geometry, faults and ACTNUM are not supported yet, and the page says so rather than guessing.

Comes out

Pressure and saturation per cell per timestep, well rates and bottom-hole pressures, cumulative production, and a material-balance ledger computed from the solution itself — not from the residual. A truncated run is refused rather than reported.

Why it exists

A second opinion you can actually open.

OPM Flow and the commercial simulators are better than this on every axis that matters for production work — speed, physics, robustness, grid complexity. This engine exists for the questions those tools cannot answer by construction.

When the number is challenged

A forecast is queried by a partner, a lender or a regulator. With a commercial simulator the answer is "the vendor's solver said so". Here the answer is a committed run, a pinned deck, a checksum, and two thousand lines anyone can read.

When the solver is the suspect

If two simulators disagree, which one is wrong? An independent implementation, written from the equations rather than from a shared codebase, is the only way to find out. That is what this is for.

When there is no licence to spare

A seat costs tens of thousands a year and stops working when the paperwork lapses. This runs on a two-core laptop with no licence server — useful in a field office, a classroom, or a regulator's desk.

When the answer has to be reproducible

Same deck, same flags, same bits, five years from now. The driver records the tolerance it used and refuses to compare a truncated run. Reproducibility is a gate here, not a promise.

What it is not. It is not a replacement for Eclipse, CMG, tNavigator or OPM Flow, and it is not faster than them. On SPE1 it is roughly ten times slower than OPM Flow at its loosest setting and far slower at the tolerance needed to pass its own mass-balance gate. Anyone selling you a from-scratch simulator as a speed upgrade is selling you something else.

This does not meet the project's own pre-registered accuracy gate (1 psi on pressure, 1e-3 on saturation). It is a 3% match on cumulative oil over nine years, and the gap is largest late in the run, where the producer is bottom-hole-pressure constrained and the gas-oil ratio diverges — exactly where relative permeability and the rate-to-BHP switch matter most. The reference is OPM Flow 2024.10, sha256-pinned, run on the same two-core laptop and step-capped at 30 days. The committed output is in the repository, so the comparison can be re-run without re-running the solver.

Honest limits

What it does not do.

  • Not a speed upgrade. Slower than OPM Flow and much slower than a commercial simulator. If speed is your problem, buy one of those.
  • The gas rate is the weak number. 18.5% maximum error on producing gas-oil ratio late in SPE1. Do not size separators or split revenue on this engine's gas numbers.
  • Cartesian grids only. No corner-point geometry, no faults, no ACTNUM, no local grid refinement. Simple DIMENS/DX/DY/DZ decks.
  • No capillary pressure, no compositional model, no thermal. Black oil with the deck's own tables.
  • Fixed-rate injection. The gas injector is modelled as a prescribed source rather than a BHP-unknown well.
  • Validated on a published benchmark, not on your field. That is what the pilot below is for.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Does it replace Eclipse, CMG or OPM Flow?

No. It is slower and covers less physics. It exists to cross-check them, to be read, and to run where a licence cannot go.

Why should I trust a 3% match?

You should not trust it as a production forecast. You should trust it as an independent implementation: the deck is pinned, the reference is pinned, the run is committed, the Jacobian is verified against dense finite differences, and the mass balance is checked every step. The misses are printed on this page, including the 18.5% gas-oil ratio error.

What do I need to run it?

Python, NumPy and SciPy. Two CPU cores, no GPU, no cluster, no licence server. It runs fully offline.

Can it read my field deck?

Only if it is a simple Cartesian deck using DIMENS, DX/DY/DZ, PERMX/Y/Z, PORO and TOPS. Corner-point geometry, faults and ACTNUM are not supported yet. Ask before assuming.

What about the gas-oil ratio gap?

It is real, measured, and stated in full: 18.5% maximum error late in SPE1, where the producer is BHP-constrained and the rate-to-pressure switch dominates. Until it closes, treat the engine as a pressure-and-cumulative-oil cross-check, not a gas forecaster.
