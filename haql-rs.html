What it is

A fast screening simulator that reads Eclipse and speaks plain numbers.

2D two-phase black-oil (oil + gas, SPE-1/PUNQ fluids, Peaceman wells), single-phase validation path, heterogeneous permeability maps, production-data fitting. One small binary, one CPU thread, fully offline.

Goes in

Your Eclipse deck (.DATA) or a handful of flags — permeability, porosity, rates, well positions. FIELD and METRIC decks; 43-point integration suite guards the import.

Comes out

Well pressures and gas saturation at 100 days, full-field CSV grids, and an evaluation report with in-place volumes and a material-balance error computed from the outputs — not hidden inside a loss function.

Why companies need it

Every current simulator taxes you three times. haql-rs charges none of them.

Eclipse, CMG, tNavigator and their peers are excellent at what they do. They also all impose the same three costs — and those costs, not accuracy, are what haql-rs removes.

The license-server tax

A commercial seat costs roughly a junior engineer's salary every year, needs a license server, and stops working the day the paperwork lapses — including in the field office and on the rig, where the questions actually arise. haql-rs is one small binary with an offline hardware-bound key. It runs where the work is.

The cluster-queue tax

A history-match loop on a sector model means rebuild the deck, submit to the cluster, wait in queue — thirty minutes to hours per iteration, dozens of iterations per match. haql-rs closes the same loop in 35 seconds on a laptop. Five what-ifs finish in the time one cluster job takes to start.

The seat-count tax

Per-seat pricing means only three people in a fifteen-engineer asset team can afford to touch the model. haql-rs site pricing covers the whole team, so the engineer with the question runs the case instead of filing a request with the engineer holding the license.

The black-box tax

When a commercial run looks wrong, you get a number and no explanation. haql-rs ships its own finite-difference reference and a validate command that prints pass and fail side by side — including the failures. Honest limits

What it is not — yet.

  • Not a GOR forecaster. Until the gas-saturation gap closes, do not size separators or split revenue on its gas numbers. Pressure-driven decisions are the validated use.
  • No BHP-controlled wells. Rate control only; BHP limits in imported decks are reported as ignored, not silently applied.
  • 2D areal. Sector screening and pattern floods, not full-field 3D development planning.
  • Validated on benchmarks, not your field — yet. Which is exactly what the pilot below is for.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Does it replace Eclipse or CMG?

No. It replaces the cluster queue in front of them. Screen five scenarios in haql-rs in three minutes, then run the winner in full physics. It is a screening tool with validated pressure accuracy, not a replacement simulator.

Why should I trust a 35-second forecast?

Because we publish the misses: the full scorecard above, the 3.6-point gas gap, and a validate command that prints pass and fail against its own reference. And because the pilot lets you test it on your field before paying for a license.

What do I need to run it?

One small binary, one CPU thread, no GPU, no cluster, no license server. Your Eclipse deck goes in unchanged. It runs fully offline, including air-gapped networks.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The binary makes no network calls. Like every Haql Labs tool, it runs where the data already is.

What about the gas saturation gap?

It is real, measured, and first on the roadmap: producer gas saturation reads 5.0% against an 8.6% reference. Until it closes, make pressure-driven decisions with haql-rs and keep gas-rate-critical work on your incumbent.
